MajoranaPropagation.ms_mult — Method
ms_mult(ms1::MajoranaString, ms2::MajoranaString)
ms_mult(ms1::TT, ms2::TT, n_fermions::Integer) where {TT<:Integer}

Multiply two Majorana strings, returning a tuple (prefactor, ms3) such that $\hat{M}_1 \hat{M}_2 = \mathrm{prefactor} \cdot \hat{M}_3$, where $\hat{M} = i^{\omega_L(ms)} \, \gamma_{k_1} \cdots \gamma_{k_w}$ is the Hermitian operator represented by a Majorana string. The string ms3 contains the Majorana operators present in exactly one of the two inputs (bitwise XOR), and prefactor is $\pm 1$ if the strings commute and $\pm i$ if they anticommute.

MajoranaPropagation.omega_L_mult — Method
omega_L_mult(ms1::MajoranaString, ms2::MajoranaString)
omega_L_mult(ms1::TT, ms2::TT, Nbits) where {TT<:Integer}
omega_L_mult(ms::MajoranaString)
omega_L_mult(ms::TT) where {TT<:Integer}

Compute the ordering parity $\omega_L(ms_1, ms_2) = v_1^T \Omega_L \, v_2 \bmod 2$, where $v_{1,2}$ are the binary vectors of the two Majorana strings and $\Omega_L$ is the strictly lower triangular all-ones matrix. This is the parity of the number of Majorana pairs $k_1 \in ms_1$, $k_2 \in ms_2$ with $k_2 < k_1$, i.e. the reordering sign picked up when merging the two ascending products. For the integer forms, Nbits is the number of Majorana modes 2 * nfermions. The one-argument form computes the self parity $\omega_L(ms) = w(w-1)/2 \bmod 2$ of a string of weight $w$, which defines the Hermitian normalization $i^{\omega_L(ms)}$ of stored Majorana strings.
